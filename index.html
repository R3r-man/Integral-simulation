Why integration by parts goes in a circle

Let I = ∫ eˣ cos x dx. Step through the two rounds of parts.

Round 1. Take u = cos x, dv = eˣ dx:
I = eˣ cos x + ∫ eˣ sin x dx
Round 2. Take u = sin x, dv = eˣ dx. The new integral is not simpler, and its cos x comes back:
∫ eˣ sin x dx = eˣ sin x − ∫ eˣ cos x dx = eˣ sin x − I
The loop closes. Substitute round 2 into round 1, and I appears on both sides:
I = eˣ cos x + eˣ sin x − I  ⟹  2I = eˣ(sin x + cos x)
I = ½ eˣ (sin x + cos x) + C

Two facts cause the cycle. Differentiating cos x twice gives −cos x, so the trig factor returns with a minus sign. Integrating eˣ never changes it. After two rounds you have the original integral times −1, so you solve for it like an ordinary equation instead of integrating further. Parts never “finishes”, which is why the algebra step is needed.
